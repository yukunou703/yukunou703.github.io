Histone modifications define distinct nucleosome positions in human cell-free DNA

Y. Ou, G. S. Gulati, M. Pandey, and Y. Ou, G. S. Gulati, M. Pandey, J.-H. Seo, Z. Zhang, G. G. Lee, Z. Jin, H. Savignano, A. G. Sowalsky, R. Nawfal, Z. Zhang, E. Ibrahim, G. Nafeh, M. L. Freedman, D. Y. Takeda, S. C. Baca (1st of 16)

bioRxiv · 2026 · Pending submission

We identify modification-associated positions (MAPs), loci at which circulating nucleosomes carrying active (H3K4me2) and repressive (H3K9me3) histone marks occupy reproducibly offset positions, and read nucleosome protection at MAPs from ordinary plasma cfDNA sequencing without antibodies to estimate tumour fraction in held-out patients, across prostate, breast and lung cancer, and across serial plasma draws. A joint project of the Baca and Freedman labs.

Research Square · 2026 · In review at Nature Genetics

My contributions: I ran the early feature-importance analysis with SHAP values to find which plasma chromatin features drive APEX’s predictions of tumour gene expression. In the final model, the strongest predictors include H3K4me3 coverage in the proximal gene body and H3K36me3 fragments of 160–200 bp. I also built the Snakemake workflow that runs APEX on high-performance computing clusters and maintain its code.

PreprintCode

APEX infers genome-wide, single-gene tumour expression from plasma chromatin and cfDNA fragmentation. Across 559 plasma samples from 24 cancer types, it resolves pancreatic cancer subtypes, tracks therapeutic targets in small-cell lung cancer and identifies NECTIN4 expression as a biomarker of response to enfortumab vedotin in urothelial cancer.

bioRxiv · 2026

My contributions: I benchmarked seven regression models, from linear models to random forests and gradient boosting, with repeated cross-validation, which led to XGBoost as the APEX model. I compared promoter and gene-body feature configurations to select the input features, and trained the final model on about 275,000 gene–sample pairs (18,342 genes in 15 patients). By adding increasing noise to the tumour RNA-seq labels, I showed that APEX stays accurate under moderate variation in the expression measurements.

In third-round review · Nucleic Acids Research

My contributions: I proposed and developed the signal attenuation score (SAS), which uses tumour-associated allelic imbalance in whole-genome sequencing to flag plasma samples in which immunoprecipitation under-represents tumour DNA. This lets users tell when weak chromatin signal reflects under-captured tumour DNA rather than the tumour’s biology. I also maintained and tested the pipeline, curated data and drew supplementary figures.
